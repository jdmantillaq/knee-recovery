# Knee Rehabilitation & Strengthening Program

**Focus:** Posterior knee pain related to the hamstrings
**Duration:** 4–6 weeks (reassess at 6 weeks)
**Frequency:** 3 training sessions/week (≈ 1 hour each) + 15 min daily warm-up/stretching routine
**Setting:** Outdoor / public park
**Equipment:** Resistance bands (light, medium, heavy loop bands + a long therapy band), yoga mat

---

## Important Notes Before Starting

- Pain during exercise should stay **≤ 3/10**. Sharp, stabbing, or radiating pain = stop and reassess.
- The hamstrings cross both the hip and the knee. Posterior knee pain often improves by **strengthening the hamstrings eccentrically** and addressing **hip and ankle weakness** that overloads the hamstrings.
- Do the **daily 15-min warm-up/stretching routine** every day, even on rest days. It is the most important part of the program.
- Hydrate, wear supportive shoes, and choose a flat grassy area for stability.
- Rest at least 24 hours between strength sessions (e.g. Mon / Wed / Fri or Tue / Thu / Sat).

## Progression Plan

| Week | What changes |
|------|--------------|
| **Week 1** | Light band, focus on form. Stay at the easy end of pain (≤ 2/10). |
| **Week 2** | Same reps, slightly heavier band OR slower eccentric (3 → 4 sec). |
| **Week 3** | Add 1 set to A1, A4, B1, C1 (now 4 × instead of 3 ×). |
| **Week 4** | Increase reps by 2 across the board; progress single-leg variations. |
| **Week 5** | Heavier band on hamstring curl, RDL, and TKE. Add light tempo holds. |
| **Week 6** | **Reassess pain, range, and strength.** If posterior knee pain is gone and hamstring strength symmetrical, transition to a maintenance program (2 sessions/week). |

---

## Red Flags — Stop and Seek Medical Review

- Sharp, sudden pain behind the knee, especially with swelling, warmth, or calf tenderness (rule out DVT).
- Locking, giving way, or clicking with pain.
- Numbness, tingling, or shooting pain down the leg.
- Pain that worsens week over week despite reduced load.
